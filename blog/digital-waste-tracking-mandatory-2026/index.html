Digital Waste Tracking Becomes Mandatory From October 2026: What Facilities Teams Need to Do

The Environment Agency's Digital Waste Tracking service went live in April 2026, and from 1 October 2026 it stops being optional for a large chunk of the sector. Paper waste transfer notes and duty of care records are being phased out in favour of electronic tracking — and while the first phase targets waste sites rather than schools or landlords directly, it changes what your waste contractors will be asking you for.

Who's affected, and when

  • Phase 1 — 1 October 2026: operators of permitted or licensed waste receiving sites in England, Wales and Northern Ireland must record the type, quantity and destination of waste movements electronically, either via an API connection or an interim spreadsheet tool. Scotland follows in January 2027.
  • Phase 2 — October 2027: waste carriers, brokers, dealers and exporters are brought into the digital system.

Most schools, community venues and commercial sites we work with aren't licensed waste sites themselves — but every one of them relies on a waste contractor who either already is one, or will be a carrier caught by Phase 2. That means the paperwork trail you're used to seeing is about to change shape.

Why this matters even if you're not directly caught

The point of the system is to close the gap that's historically let waste crime and fly-tipping-by-paperwork slip through — a skip that's supposed to go to a licensed site but doesn't always get there. As your contractors move onto digital tracking, you should expect:

  • Digital confirmation of where your waste actually ended up, rather than a stack of paper transfer notes
  • Contractors asking you to confirm site details and waste classifications more precisely, since inaccurate data now flows straight into a regulator-visible system
  • Less tolerance for informal or undocumented waste arrangements — the "mate with a van" approach becomes a much bigger liability

What to do before October 2026

  1. Ask your current waste contractor which phase applies to them and how they're preparing for the transition.
  2. Review your existing waste transfer note records for gaps — missing notes, unclear waste classifications, or contractors you can't currently verify as licensed.
  3. If you operate a permitted or licensed waste site yourself (for example, on-site skip storage under a specific licence), check whether you fall into Phase 1 and need to register for the digital service directly.
  4. Build waste documentation into your existing compliance calendar alongside your other statutory records, rather than treating it as a standalone admin task.
  5. Keep an eye on WEEE and lithium-battery waste specifically — IT equipment, vape and e-bike battery disposal is an area regulators are watching closely, and it sits squarely inside this same tightening of waste oversight.
