Getting Contractor and Visitor Inductions Right

Ask most site managers whether they have a visitor and contractor induction process, and the answer is usually yes. Ask what it actually covers beyond a signature in a book, and the answer is often much thinner. A proper induction is one of the cheapest, most effective risk controls available — and one of the most commonly done badly.

Why the sign-in book isn't enough

A signature confirms someone was on site. It doesn't confirm they know where the fire assembly point is, which areas are off-limits, who to report a hazard to, or what to do if there's an incident while they're there. If something goes wrong, "they signed in" is a weak position to be defending.

What a proper induction covers

  • Fire and emergency procedures — assembly point, alarm sound, evacuation routes relevant to where they'll be
  • Site-specific hazards — vehicle movements, restricted areas, asbestos locations if relevant, any live works in progress
  • Who's responsible for them — a named host or contact, not "someone in the office"
  • Reporting — how to raise a hazard, incident or near-miss while on site
  • Access boundaries — exactly where they're authorised to be, and where they're not
  • PPE and site rules specific to the work being carried out, where relevant

Contractors need more than visitors do

A delivery driver dropping off a parcel needs the basics. A contractor working unsupervised on site for a week needs considerably more: sight of relevant risk assessments and method statements, confirmation of their own competence and insurance, and briefing on how their work interacts with anything else happening on site that day. Treating every visitor the same, regardless of what they're actually there to do, is where inductions start to lose their value.

Making it practical, not painful

An induction that takes twenty minutes and feels like a chore will get skipped the second time. The sites that get this right keep the induction focused and specific — a short briefing plus a simple written record, tailored to whether it's a one-off visitor or a contractor on site for an extended period — rather than a single generic form trying to cover every scenario at once.
