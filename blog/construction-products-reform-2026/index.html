Construction Products Reform: What the General Safety Requirement Means for Fire Doors and Safety-Critical Products

On 26 February 2026, the Government published its Construction Products Reform White Paper, alongside a consultation on a new General Safety Requirement (GSR) for construction products. It's an early-stage proposal rather than a live duty — but it's a direct response to the Grenfell Tower Inquiry, the Hackitt Review and the Morrell-Day Review, and it names fire doors specifically as a priority category. If you specify, install or maintain them, it's worth understanding where this is heading.

What's actually being proposed

Two strands sit underneath the White Paper:

  • A General Safety Requirement (GSR) — a new statutory baseline covering the estimated 66% of construction products currently not covered by any designated standard or technical assessment. Today, if a product falls outside existing regulation, there's effectively no mandatory safety requirement attached to it at all. The GSR closes that gap.
  • Enhanced requirements for "safety-critical" products — for products the Government considers critical to safe construction, fire doors are explicitly named as an example, additional measures will apply where adherence to a standard alone isn't considered sufficient to demonstrate safety.

The GSR is intended to be brought into force using existing powers under the Building Safety Act 2022, which is why it sits alongside — rather than instead of — the other BSA-driven changes already reshaping fire door and building safety compliance this year.

Timing: what's confirmed and what isn't

Nothing here is in force yet. The White Paper and GSR consultation were published in February 2026; the Government's stated intention is to introduce regulations by the end of 2026, with implementation following in late 2027. Expect further consultation and draft regulations before anything becomes a live duty on site — but the direction of travel is clear, and it reinforces the same theme running through fire door standards, the Building Safety Regulator and Approved Document B this year: safety-critical products and documentation are being pulled into a tighter, more evidence-based regime.

Why this matters alongside your existing fire door work

If you've already reviewed your fire door documentation against BS 8214:2026 or looked at the Building Safety Regulator's wider oversight in our Golden Thread post, this is the next layer up: it's less about how a fire door is inspected and maintained on your site, and more about the safety evidence a manufacturer has to hold before that door is ever specified or installed. For building owners and Responsible Persons, the practical upshot is the same principle repeating — documentation and evidence trails for safety-critical products are only going to get more scrutinised, not less.

What to do now

  1. Nothing is mandatory yet — there's no new duty to action today. This is a "watch this space" item, not an urgent compliance task.
  2. If you're specifying fire doors or other safety-critical products for new works, keep the supplier's safety evidence and certification on file now — it puts you ahead of where the GSR is heading rather than scrambling to catch up later.
  3. Fold this into your Golden Thread documentation alongside your BS 8214:2026 and Building Safety Regulator records, so it's one consistent evidence trail rather than several disconnected ones.
  4. We'll update this page and the compliance changes summary as the consultation progresses and draft regulations are published.
