Crime and Policing Act 2026: What the Safeguarding Changes Mean for Schools and Nurseries

From 1 September 2026, changes introduced through the Crime and Policing Act 2026 remove the long-standing supervision exemption from the definition of "regulated activity" — and it's volunteers, not staff, who feel the impact first.

What the supervision exemption used to allow

Under the old rules, unpaid activity such as teaching, training, instructing, or caring for or supervising children didn't count as regulated activity if it was supervised by someone who was themselves in regulated activity — a class teacher overseeing a parent helper, for example. That supervision was enough to keep the volunteer outside the regulated activity definition, and outside the enhanced DBS requirement that comes with it.

What changes from 1 September 2026

The supervision exemption goes. From that date, a volunteer teaching, training or supervising children more than three days in any 30-day period, or overnight, is in regulated activity regardless of whether a staff member is supervising them. That means an enhanced DBS check with children's barred list information becomes a requirement, not an option.

This applies across every setting working with children — schools, nurseries, pre-schools and childminding arrangements alike.

Who's actually affected

Staff who are already DBS-checked at the enhanced level won't notice much difference. The people this catches are:

  • Regular volunteer helpers — parent helpers, reading volunteers, sports coaches — who currently rely on staff supervision rather than holding their own enhanced check
  • Governors or trustees who spend regular time on site in a way that meets the frequency or overnight condition
  • Work experience placements and student volunteers on extended or overnight placements

What to do before 1 September

  1. Audit your current volunteer list against the new frequency and overnight tests — don't assume last year's list still holds.
  2. Identify anyone relying on the supervision exemption and get their enhanced DBS application moving now, not in August.
  3. Update your single central record and safeguarding policy to reflect the removed exemption.
  4. Brief governors, trustees and regular volunteers on what's changing and why, so nobody is caught out at the start of the new academic year.
  5. Fold this into your wider September readiness checklist alongside Martyn's Law and any other site-access changes you're already planning for.

This sits close to premises management as much as it does safeguarding policy — visitor and volunteer access, sign-in procedures and site supervision all need to reflect the new position.
